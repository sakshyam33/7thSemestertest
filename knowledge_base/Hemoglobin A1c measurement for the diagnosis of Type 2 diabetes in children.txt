Hemoglobin A1c measurement for the diagnosis of Type 2 diabetes in children evaluates the utility, reliability, and methodology of using laboratory-based HbA1c testing for diagnosing Type 2 diabetes in pediatric populations, addressing recent studies that dismissed HbA1c due to poor correlation with Oral Glucose Tolerance Tests (OGTT) or Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG). The authors find that rejecting HbA1c based on lack of correlation with OGTT or FPG is scientifically flawed because neither OGTT nor FPG have themselves been validated against long-term health outcomes (such as retinopathy) in children, and OGTT suffers from low reproducibility. Additionally, non-fasting HbA1c screening is more practical, reproducible, and has been shown to increase diabetes screening rates and early detection among adolescents. The paper recommends that American Diabetes Association (ADA) diagnostic criteria remain useful for screening at-risk, asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic pediatric populations, with laboratory-based HbA1c certified by the National Glycohemoglobin Standardization Program (NGSP) being the preferred method; practitioners should exercise clinical judgment when diagnostic test results conflict, and HbA1c should be avoided in cases of pregnancy, cystic fibrosis, or hemoglobinopathies and disorders causing high red blood cell turnover.
