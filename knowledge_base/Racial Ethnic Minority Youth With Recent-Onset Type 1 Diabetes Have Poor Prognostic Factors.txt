Racial/Ethnic Minority Youth With Recent-Onset Type 1 Diabetes Have Poor Prognostic Factors evaluates the demographic and clinical differences at type 1 diabetes diagnosis and during the first three years post-diagnosis among non-Hispanic white, Hispanic, and African American youth under 19 years of age to understand variations in long-term health outcomes. The findings reveal that African American participants presented with higher BMI percentiles, more advanced pubertal development, a higher frequency of diabetic ketoacidosis at diagnosis, and higher rates of hypertension and severe hypoglycemia over the three-year follow-up, while being less likely to enter partial remission. Additionally, both Hispanic and African American youth maintained trajectories with higher insulin doses, higher IDAA1c, and higher BMI percentiles compared to non-Hispanic white youth, with Hispanics also demonstrating a higher prevalence of dyslipidemia.
