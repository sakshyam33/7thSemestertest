Screening and Diagnosis of Prediabetes and Diabetes in US Children and Adolescents evaluates the performance of American Diabetes Association risk-based screening criteria and current clinical definitions of prediabetes and diabetes—based on HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), or both—to identify high cardiometabolic risk in US youth aged 10 to 19 years. The study found that while 25.5% of US youth met screening criteria, the sensitivity and specificity of these criteria were low for detecting hyperglycemia. Confirmed undiagnosed diabetes was rare (less than 0.5% of youth), with over 85% of diabetes cases already diagnosed. Furthermore, HbA1c-defined hyperglycemia showed significantly stronger and more specific associations with cardiometabolic risk compared to FPG-defined hyperglycemia. Based on these findings, the study highlights HbA1c as a highly specific, effective nonfasting test to identify high-risk youth who could benefit from lifestyle interventions to prevent diabetes and cardiovascular risks later in life.
