Disturbed Eating Behavior and Omission of Insulin in Adolescents Receiving Intensified Insulin Treatment evaluates the prevalence of disturbed eating behavior (DEB) and insulin restriction or omission among 770 Norwegian adolescents aged 11–19 with type 1 diabetes using the Diabetes Eating Problem Survey-Revised (DEPS-R). The findings indicate that 27.7% of females and 8.6% of males scored above the threshold for DEB, with prevalence increasing significantly alongside higher age and weight categories (reaching 38.1% in the oldest age group and 32.7% among obese participants). Additionally, 31.6% of participants reported restricting insulin and 6.9% reported omitting insulin entirely after overeating. Both DEB and insulin restriction were significantly associated with higher HbA1c levels (9.2% vs. 8.4% for DEB; 9.0% vs. 8.3% for restriction), highlighting poorer metabolic control and elevated risk for long-term diabetes complications. The paper emphasizes the need for early identification and further clinical assessment of at-risk individuals, as well as the importance of early intervention to prevent the development of full-threshold eating disorders and mitigate severe health risks.
