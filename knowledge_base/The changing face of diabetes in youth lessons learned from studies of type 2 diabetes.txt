The changing face of diabetes in youth: lessons learned from studies of type 2 diabetes evaluates multicenter clinical and epidemiological studies—including TODAY, SEARCH, and HEALTHY—to examine the epidemiology, pathophysiology, diagnosis, treatment, and complications of type 2 diabetes in youth. Findings demonstrate that youth-onset type 2 diabetes is characterized by severe insulin resistance and a rapid decline in pancreatic β-cell function that progresses three to four times faster than in adults, leading to significantly higher therapeutic failure rates with standard metformin treatment. The disease disproportionately affects minority racial and ethnic groups as well as female adolescents, with rising pediatric obesity clouding the clinical distinction between obese type 1 diabetes and type 2 diabetes. To address this growing health crisis, the authors highlight guidelines and recommendations for urgent development of novel, approved pharmacotherapeutic agents that target pediatric pathophysiological mechanisms alongside aggressive lifestyle and community-based prevention initiatives to halt the projected fourfold increase in youth type 2 diabetes by 2050.
